# Stations & embassies

> Physical places that support participation and shared work.

Stations proposes local spaces for learning, collaboration, tools, care and infrastructure. It also distinguishes those community functions from the separate proposal for institutional embassies.

A station would be shaped around local activities and needs, linking people and resources to the wider network. Embassy proposals concern negotiated institutional cooperation and, most of the time, an autonomous zone on territory a country cedes.

Physical presence gives the distributed system a relationship with the places and communities it is intended to serve.

## A practical example

A local group could begin with regular study and workshop sessions in an available space, then assess which shared tools and support would make the activity sustainable. This is an illustration of the proposed design.

## The problem it addresses

Participation depends on physical and social conditions as well as connectivity. People may need a place to meet, equipment to use or practical support to begin.

## Where this stands

Local posts and stations are territorial structures for work, formation, access to advanced equipment, laboratories and mutual support; they are places where tools and hardware are kept, where members pass on what they have learned and where projects receive physical support that no individual could keep up alone; their usefulness is measured by the real capacity people build there. A station does not need to be an autonomous zone: it is a Drayker property anywhere in the world, much like an office for a given purpose, from welcoming members and study to research and work. Embassies belong to another scale. They come from agreements with countries and governments, for cooperation in research, technological capacity, industrial modernization, climate security and disaster preparedness, and most of the time an embassy is an autonomous zone on ceded territory. It is not a simple concession: with the support of its people and the consent of its state, a country cedes territory that becomes an autonomous zone, independent, almost another country, often still bordering other states. Pure autonomous zones are at sea. On the high seas no one needs to cede territory, and that is their great advantage. Autonomous zones, at sea or on ceded territory, include housing for the people who live and work there. A whole country can also adopt the Drayker infrastructure in a local and customized version that runs isolated from the global network. Each person in the country has their own UID in that system. Using that system does not make anyone a member: each person chooses whether to make their UID also a Drayker member UID. Whoever does has the two integrated, almost like dual citizenship. That is where the name comes from: an embassy becomes a sovereign territory of Drayker bordering other countries, and its conditions, guarantees and responsibilities are set in the concrete bilateral agreements. Territorial embassies are distinct from the Drayker Embassy, the founding core described in [GOVERNANCE.md](https://github.com/draykerdk/.github/blob/master/GOVERNANCE.md).

Drayker also has internal material on stations and support centres that is not published yet. What a station commits to, and how it relates to the support layer of the network, are still open.

The internal material describes possibilities, not a network of operating locations. There is no inventory of properties, no staff, no budget, no licences and no confirmed pilot. This is long-term work rather than something waiting on a decision.

This repository develops the proposal through public documentation and review. The capabilities described here still require specifications, worked examples and implementation.

## How a station comes to be

A station is created like any project on the [projects platform](https://pap.drayker.org). Any group of members can propose one, with its kind (study, research, data, network, contribution or production) and its purpose. It starts as an idea with objectives and purposes agreed among its participants, the resources it needs and a plan that develops in discussion. Roles are given, participation is defined, and the place grows by levels and milestones: an existing space, rented, bought collectively or donated, or a building made for it. The pattern encouraged is a station that sustains itself through its own activity. Like any project, it receives common capacity according to the value it delivers and the promise it shows, and what it learns is recorded in its own Dknowledge. In the wells example of the [projects platform](https://pap.drayker.org), a town gains a post with a spectrometer none of the towns would buy alone, because the work there had already shown what it delivered.

## Where this sits in time

Today no station, post or embassy exists. A station can begin today as a project with a public form. Posts with shared equipment follow as fronts of work show what they deliver. The first territorial embassy belongs to the consolidation phase that the 2033 objective names. Pure autonomous zones on the high seas come later.

## An embassy, not an enclave

An embassy does not take land from a people. It exists only with the support of the local population and the agreement of the state. Its purpose is cooperation in research, technological capacity, industrial modernization, climate security and disaster preparedness, never extraction. In the design, an embassy holds research and development laboratories, the clinics of Autonomous Health, housing, Academy centres, and areas where new projects are tested and validated with participants. It is a barrier against domination: a country's superintelligence is built by its own people instead of arriving ready from whoever dominates it. People affected from outside, who never chose to take part, receive accessible information, representation, negotiation of conditions and means of repair. On the high seas no one needs to cede territory at all. The conditions, guarantees and responsibilities of each embassy are set in the agreement that creates it. The agreement that creates each embassy states beforehand what happens if the local population or a later government withdraws its consent, including how the people living there are protected.

The contrast is real. Próspera, in Honduras, was presented as a zone for employment and economic development. After Congress repealed the law that created such zones in 2022, the company claimed about 10.8 billion US dollars from the country at the World Bank's arbitration centre, under the CAFTA-DR trade agreement, and on 20 September 2024 the Supreme Court declared the zones unconstitutional, by eight votes to seven.

Sources: [Claimants' observations on the respondent's preliminary objection](https://www.italaw.com/sites/default/files/case-documents/italaw182537.pdf), *Honduras Próspera Inc. and others v. Republic of Honduras*, ICSID Case No. ARB/23/2, 26 September 2024; [the tribunal's decision on the preliminary objections](https://www.italaw.com/sites/default/files/case-documents/italaw1826660.pdf) in the same case, 26 February 2025; Marcia Perdomo, ["Crypto Bros Are Trying to Bankrupt Honduras for Scuttling Their Private Cities"](https://fpif.org/crypto-bros-are-trying-to-bankrupt-honduras-for-scuttling-their-private-cities/), Foreign Policy in Focus, 6 February 2025.

## Scope

- Stations as a project model
- The distinction between local posts and stations, support centres and embassies
- A station begins as a project, and its place follows from what the project already delivers and needs
- Production, research and service variants
- Relation to distributed support, Academy and the economy
- Local emergence inside one global body

## Not in scope

- An existing centre, embassy or physical facility.
- A property, residency or relocation offer available today.
- A date for any particular place, or a place promised to anyone. The first embassy belongs to the consolidation phase that the 2033 objective names ([Direction](https://dknowledge.drayker.org/roadmap/DIRECTION)).

## How it fits the whole

Where the ecosystem exists physically — the places where a network of people who may never meet in person finally meets.

Stations are a model on the [projects platform](https://pap.drayker.org), organised like any other project. They may become local physical and social fronts of the wider [Distributed Support](https://support.drayker.org) program, but they are not the whole program and support need not require a place. Their needs, resources and consequences belong to the broader capacity economy and member governance; [DAF](https://daf.drayker.org) may test limited rules during the transition, while [Dktron/value units](https://value.drayker.org) are proposed instruments for distributing resources and connecting to external economies. A station could eventually host Academy activities, laboratories, open-science work or material support, but none of those facilities, clinics, residences or travel programmes exists today. An organization with nowhere to be reaches only the people who were already online — stations are one proposed way the network could touch the ground.

## First functions

These are concrete and unclaimed. Any of them can be opened as an issue and delivered
by one person.

1. Design one pilot activity, local or remote: who coordinates it, what support it
   offers, how participation is measured, and when it ends. It needs no place at all.
2. Write what a station has to commit to.
3. Write what a station project has to show, in delivery and in self-sustaining
   activity, before common capacity goes into a place.
4. Map a station onto the distributed support layer.
